🔍 What We Test Same object in a new scene must beat a different object in the same scene
The idea, in one example
Can a model tell this alien from a different one?

Show a model a reference image. Then show it the same character somewhere new, and a different character standing in the reference's own scene. A good identity model should say the first one is the match. Most say the second, because it recognises the street, not the character.

REFERENCE

The character we care about.

SAME · NEW SCENE

Same character, everything else changed. This is the match.

DIFFERENT · SAME SCENE

Different character, identical street. Background is now useless as a clue.

Pass the test only if same · new scene scores higher than different · same scene.

The whole benchmark is this test, 500 identities wide and nine distractor generators deep. Because the distractor sits in the reference's own background, a model cannot score well by matching the scene – the only usable evidence is the object itself. SSR is the fraction of identities where every such comparison comes out right. Similarity values above are cosine scores from the released checkpoints on the held-out test split.

◎ Two Axes High SSR is easy to fake – human alignment is what keeps it honest

Discrimination is not alignment. Every point is a scoring model placed by its NearID-bench SSR (x, the test above) and its DreamBench++ M–H (y) – how well its scores correlate with human judgements of personalized generations. NearID (blue) sits top-right at 99.17 / 0.545, above the frozen SigLIP2 baseline on both axes. The amber band is the warning: training-objective variants that reach 99.6–100 SSR while human alignment falls to 0.14–0.25. A metric can win the benchmark by collapsing its representation, which is why we never report SSR on its own.

On the two purple points. ID-Sim (CVPR 2026) and TPIPS (arXiv 2026) are concurrent identity and perceptual-similarity metrics, not part of the paper. We scored them ourselves under the NearID protocol after publication, and we report each at its best configuration: ID-Sim at 53.11 SSR (best of 15 backbone × scoring-mode combinations) and TPIPS at 34.89 SSR (best of 14 text factors). Both reproduce their own published numbers in our hands, so this is a difference in objective, not an integration failure. They lead us on human alignment and trail us on matched-context discrimination – the ordering on the two axes is reversed, which is the same point the amber band makes from the other direction.

⚖ Paradigm Contrastive framework with explicit structured near-identity distractors

(A) SimCLR: augmented copies of one real image. (B) StableRep: multiple diffusion samples from the same text prompt. (C) NearID (Ours): 3D-consistent multi-view images of the same identity, produced via depth-conditioned generation and cross-view feature warping from Objaverse 3D assets (SynCD); a different but visually similar instance inpainted into the same background as the structured negative.

(A) SimCLR

Strong stochastic augmentations of one real image form the positive pair. Learns pixel-level invariance; no semantic distractors are present.

(B) StableRep

A diffusion model generates multiple images from the same text caption. Learns caption-level invariance but cannot distinguish intra-class instances.

(C) NearID (Ours)

Positives are 3D-consistent multi-view images of the same object identity, produced via depth-conditioned diffusion generation and cross-view feature warping from Objaverse 3D assets (SynCD). Near-identity distractors – a different but visually similar instance inpainted into the exact same background – are the key negative signal absent from both SimCLR and StableRep.

🎬 KPCA Evolution Positives cluster, distractors separate over 3300 training steps

What to look for

In the SigLIP2 baseline (frozen), positives and near-identity distractors of the same identity are interleaved – the embedding cannot tell them apart.

As NearID training progresses, same-identity positives (•) collapse into tight clusters while distractors (✖) are pushed to distinct regions – even though they share the exact same background as the anchor.

  • Step 0: SigLIP2 frozen – distractors and positives overlap
  • Steps 100–900: identity clusters begin to form
  • Steps 1000–2500: distractors progressively rejected
  • Step 3300 (final): tight positive clusters, clear separation

7 identities, val split. KernelPCA (cosine), Procrustes-aligned to final frame, global axis limits. 35 frames at 180 ms/frame.
